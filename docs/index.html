NEAR Intents X-Ray: who is on the other side of $126M a day?

Most NEAR theses quote one published volume number. This one opens the contract. We drew a seeded random sample of 720 block windows of intents.near across 29 days, downloaded every transaction in them (22,323), priced 99.7% of the token legs, and tested six claims against thresholds fixed in advance. Then we did it again: an independent second sample of 360 windows (11,284 transactions) with a different seed, to see which results survive.

Read it as a research note: report.html · PDF, 12 pages (takeaways, 15 exhibits, scenarios, risk register, questions for the team).

A test is “supported” or “contradicted” only when the whole pooled 95% interval is on one side of the threshold and both samples' own point estimates are on that side; otherwise it is “inconclusive”. 1. Is the volume real?

Value entering the contract in our sample is 0.79× (95% CI 0.57× to 1.06×) of the volume NEAR publishes for the same 28 days. The headline number is not an artefact of a dashboard: money of that order shows up on-chain. A second check agrees: the sample's transaction count is 0.97× the exact count. The independent replication sample measures 0.66× (95% CI 0.49× to 0.84×); pooling both samples gives 0.70× (0.56× to 0.84×). The verdict holds.

Daily value: published vs measured on-chain

Sample deposits average $109.4M a day (95% CI $76.8M to $149.4M) against $128.9M published; withdrawals are 0.68× (95% CI 0.50× to 0.93×) of published volume. Single days are noisy (correlation with the published series +0.19): one large deposit in a sampled window moves a day's point a lot.

How this is computed

Deposits are nep245:mt_mint events and withdrawals are mt_burn events emitted by intents.near, valued at each day's USD price. Each UTC day uses only that day's windows, scaled by blocks per day ÷ blocks sampled. Published volume is DefiLlama's NEAR Intents series, which reads NEAR's own Dune table. Daily points rest on ~24 short windows, so they are noisy; the 30-day ratio is the test.

Transactions touching intents.near per day

The sample estimates 114,889 transactions a day. Where exact counts are available the sample is 0.97× the true figure, which is the check that the sampling works.

How this is computed

Exact counts come from the txs_count field of the FastNEAR transactions API for each UTC day (scripts/fetch_public.py). The sample estimate is transactions in sampled windows times the expansion factor.

Six months of usage: exact daily transactions, 180 days

Exact counts for 180 days. The contract handled about 56,619 transactions a day in the first 30 days of this window (2026-05-18 onwards) and 113,293 a day in the last 30: 2.0× as many. Published volume over the same two spans went from $85M to $127M a day (1.5×). The busiest day was 2026-11-05 with 279,561 transactions.

How this is computed

The FastNEAR transactions API returns the exact number of transactions touching an account for any block range. We located the first block of each UTC day and asked for the count between consecutive days (scripts/fetch_long_counts.py). This is a full count, not a sample. Published volume is DefiLlama's daily series.

2. Who holds the ledger? Mostly the confidential treasury

The largest sender moves 40.6% (95% CI 34.1% to 46.4%) of the value, so the test as written passes: no single account sends half. But read it with this: that account is on one side or the other of 86.7% (95% CI 78.2% to 92.6%) of the value, and it is not an anonymous whale. NEAR's own documentation names it as the Confidential Intents Treasury: a transfer into it is the "shield" step that moves funds onto the confidential ledger, and a transfer out of it is the unshield. Every one of the 5,964 sampled transfers into it carries a long opaque memo (120 to 184 characters), and none of the 14,566 other transfers does. That matches the documentation, which says the shield memo carries the encrypted recipient. We stored only the memo's length; the one we re-fetched in full is base64 data. The independent replication sample measures 52.9% (95% CI 43.6% to 62.6%); pooling both samples gives 44.2% (39.1% to 49.4%). The two samples' point estimates fall on opposite sides of the threshold, so the claim is marked inconclusive even though the pooled interval passes.

Value shielded into Confidential Intents per day

About $85M a day is shielded into Confidential Intents ($59M to $116M), in roughly 30,695 transfers a day. Swaps that settle in the open on the public contract come to about $27M a day. So of the value we can see being settled either way, 77.6% (95% CI 71.5% to 83.7%) goes to the confidential side (two samples pooled). In the main sample the share was 59% in the earlier 15 days and 88% in the later 15.

How this is computed

Shielded value is the USD value of dip4:transfer events whose receiver is 51e8f9…7cbe. The account is identified from NEAR's documentation, which lists it as PRIVATE_TREASURY_ACCOUNT_ID, ‘Treasury account for asset wrapping’, and describes the transfer into it as the shield step (https://docs.near-intents.org/integration/market-makers/confidential-example). Public swaps are dip4:token_diff events on intents.near, each swap counted once. Solvers also shield their own liquidity to fill confidential swaps: 37% of shielded value comes from named accounts, the rest from implicit accounts, so shielded value is an upper bound on confidential swap size, not a measure of it.

Why this matters for the thesis

For. Privacy is something NEAR Intents sells that most cross-chain venues do not, and the flow has moved to it quickly. Shielded value alone is 0.55× (95% CI 0.42× to 0.68×) of the published daily volume.

Against. Per NEAR's documentation the confidential swap itself executes against a separate private contract (intents.far), not on intents.near. From the public chain we can count what goes in and what comes out, but not who traded what with whom. As this share grows, the published volume becomes a figure outsiders can check less and less.

And the take moved too. NEAR's take was 4.9 bps of published volume before the break and 4.0 bps after; what users paid went from 15.5 bps to 15.1 bps. The timing matches; we have no evidence of cause.

Also. The treasury account backs every shielded balance, so it is a custody concentration by design. Who holds its keys, and under what controls, is not something the chain shows. It is the first question we would ask the team.

When swaps left the public contract

Up to 2026-10-30, 21.8% (95% CI 19.7% to 23.8%) of transactions carried a swap settled in the open. From 2026-10-31 it is 3.5% (95% CI 2.8% to 4.3%). Open swaps fell from about $38M to $16M a day while shielded value rose from $54M to $113M a day. The share has been under 3% on every one of the last 10 full days (since 2026-11-04), with open swaps averaging $2M a day. Re-drawing the sample 1,000 times puts the break between 2026-10-30 and 2026-11-03 in 95% of draws.

How this is computed

For each day: sampled transactions with at least one dip4:token_diff event, divided by all sampled transactions that day. The break date (2026-10-31) is the split of the daily series that minimises within-period variance of the share of transactions carrying a dip4:token_diff event, with at least 5 days on each side. Intervals are window bootstraps within each period.

Open swaps vs shielded value, per day

Published volume did not fall with it: $126M a day before, $132M after. Swaps we can see in the open were 30% of the published figure before the break and 12% after. The rest of the published number now has to be taken on trust.

How this is computed

Open swaps: USD value of dip4:token_diff events, each swap counted once. Shielded: USD value of transfers into the treasury. Both are sample estimates scaled to a full day, so single days are noisy; compare the two periods, not two days. The published figure is DefiLlama's daily volume for NEAR Intents. We have not seen how that figure is built, so we report the gap and do not claim to know what fills it.

What the treasury holds right now

At block 224,532,448 (2026-11-14 15:23 UTC) the treasury held $129M on the public contract, in 174 assets; 33% of it stablecoins. About $113M a day has been shielded since the break, so the pool turns over roughly every 1.1 days.

How this is computed

One view call, mt_batch_balance_of on intents.near for the treasury account across the 231 assets in the 1Click list, valued at the list price fetched in the same run (scripts/treasury.py). Bars are each asset's share of the total.

Can the reserves be checked? Only one side

Assets: yes. Anyone can read the treasury's holdings on the public contract at any block, as we did.

Liabilities: no. The claims against those holdings are the shielded balances on the private contract. Their total is not published, so nobody outside can confirm that holdings cover claims.

Our sample cannot fill the gap. Sampled inflow minus outflow is $10M a day, but each side is uncertain by tens of millions a day, so the difference says nothing reliable about the balance. A real check needs either a full count of every transfer or NEAR publishing the shielded supply.

Who shields: many accounts, two big solvers

2,770 different accounts shielded funds in the sampled windows. Two solver accounts, solver-priv-liq.near and solver-multichain-asset.near, supply 36% of the shielded value between them; the ten largest shielders together account for 61.2% (95% CI 44.7% to 72.8%). Accounts that each contribute under 1% make up 34%. So use is broad by count, with solver liquidity as the largest single block.

How this is computed

Each transfer into the treasury is credited to its sender. Shares are of total shielded value, with 95% window-bootstrap intervals. Accounts seen in a single window have intervals that reach zero: one large transfer was sampled, and we cannot say how often that account shields. Named accounts are labelled by their on-chain name only.

How to read the confidential numbers

What is measured. Value crossing the boundary between the public contract and the treasury, in both directions, and what the treasury holds.

What is inferred. That a transfer into the treasury is the first step of a confidential swap. This rests on NEAR's documentation and on every sampled transfer into the treasury carrying a long opaque memo that no other transfer has, not on seeing the swap or reading the memo.

What is unknown. How many shielded dollars are swapped, how often, between whom, and at what price. Nothing in this report estimates those.

5. Who submits the transactions?

Five accounts submit 97.5% (95% CI 97.2% to 97.7%) of every transaction that touches the contract. End users almost never sign a NEAR transaction themselves (1.6% of transactions are signed by an unnamed user account). The independent replication sample measures 97.8% (95% CI 97.4% to 98.1%); pooling both samples gives 97.6% (97.4% to 97.8%). The verdict holds.

Share of transactions by signer

109 distinct signers in the sample; the top five submit 97.5%.

How this is computed

The signer_id of every sampled transaction.

What this means

Chain abstraction works as advertised: the user signs on their own chain and a relayer puts the result on NEAR. The cost is that liveness and censorship resistance for this flow rest on a short list of relayer keys, not on NEAR's validator set alone. For an investor this is an operational-risk line, and a decentralisation roadmap item to track.

When it is used (UTC hour)

The busiest eight hours, 09:00 to 17:00 UTC, carry 39% of transactions; an even spread would be 33%. Weekends carry 24%, against 29% if every day in the sample were equal. The hour-by-hour line is noisy, so only the broad tilt is worth reading.

How this is computed

Each sampled transaction is counted in the UTC hour of its block. Every window carries the same weight, so plain shares are unbiased for the period.

Why timing matters

Transactions lean a little towards 09:00–17:00 UTC and away from weekends. Software alone would not slow down at weekends, so this hints at people behind the five relayers. It is a weak hint: each hour rests on about 30 windows, single busy windows move the line, and the end users never appear as the signer here.

All three samples put deposits below the published volume. “Whales removed” is not a fair test of T1, because large deposits are about half of the value by construction; it is shown for the share tests only.

9. Move the goalposts yourself

Disagree with a threshold? Drag it. The verdict is recomputed in your browser with the same rule: the pooled interval must be wholly on one side, and both samples' point estimates must agree.

10. What would change our mind

  • T1 fails on a re-sample (deposits wholly outside 0.5×–2× of published volume): the demand story is not verifiable and the thesis falls.
  • The top five signers fall below 90% of transactions: the access dependency is easing.
  • Public swaps return to the public contract, or the tracker's shielded share falls back below 90% (prediction P2): the audit gap was temporary.
  • NEAR publishes verifiable aggregates for the confidential ledger (volume, solver count, treasury controls): the audit gap closes and the confidential share becomes a plain positive.
  • NEAR's captured revenue stays above 5 bps of volume for three months without volume falling: the value-capture objection is answered.
  • One chain rises above half of deposits: the product has become a single-corridor bridge and should be valued as one.

11. Method and limits

  • Sampling. The 720 most recent strata of 6,000 blocks before height 224,510,585; one window of 40 consecutive blocks per stratum, position drawn with seed 20261114. 720 windows were fully downloaded and used.
  • It is a sample. Shares carry intervals; totals are estimates. Rare, very large transfers make value shares noisier than count shares.
  • Prices. 99.0% of token legs are valued at the asset's USD price on the day of the transaction (DefiLlama daily series); the rest use the 1Click list price at fetch time. Intraday moves are not captured. The robustness table shows the effect of using a single price list instead.
  • Accounts are not people. One account is identified from NEAR's public documentation (the Confidential Intents Treasury). Every other account is described by behaviour only; we do not claim to know who operates it.
  • The confidential side is seen from outside only. We measure value entering and leaving the treasury on the public contract. What happens on the private contract is taken from NEAR's documentation, not observed.
  • Two settlement paths. 78% of trade events in the sample are transfer intents and 22% are token_diff swaps. Tests T3 and T4 are measured on the transfer path ($184.2M a day of internal movement, which counts each hop); token_diff is reported separately in section 2.
  • Deposits are not swaps. T1 compares value entering the contract with published swap volume. They are different things that should be of the same order; a ratio near 1 is not expected, a ratio of 0.1 or 10 would be a problem.
  • Thresholds. Fixed after a 6-window pilot and before the full sample was drawn, and not changed afterwards.
  • The combining rule came later. The rule for combining the main and replication samples (pooled interval, both point estimates on the same side) was written after both samples were drawn. Under a stricter rule, where each sample must pass on its own, T1 would also be inconclusive, because the smaller replication sample's interval starts at 0.49×, just under the 0.5× bound.
  • Pooling. Pooled figures are an inverse-variance average of the two samples using a normal approximation to each bootstrap interval. Where the two samples disagree (T2, T4) the pooled interval is too narrow, which is the reason for the second condition.
  • Not run. The BigQuery cross-checks in sql/ were written but not executed (no billing account). The Intents Explorer API needs a partner JWT and was not used.
  • Research, not investment advice.

13. Primer, glossary and sources

New to NEAR Intents? A user on another chain deposits, the public contract keeps the ledger, and swaps are settled either in the open or, increasingly, on a private contract behind one treasury account. Relayers submit nearly every transaction.

Solid boxes are things this project reads directly from the chain. The dashed box is described in NEAR's documentation and cannot be observed from outside.

Glossary

TermMeaning
IntentA signed statement of the outcome a user wants, such as “at least X of token B for Y of token A”.
SolverA market maker that fills intents from its own inventory.
Relayer / signerThe account that submits a transaction and pays the network fee. Test T5 measures how few of them there are.
Shield / unshieldMoving funds into or out of the confidential side through the treasury account.
Open swapA swap settled on the public contract (a token_diff event), where anyone can see who gave what for what.
Basis point (bps)One hundredth of a percent. 5 bps of $100 is 5 cents.
TakeThe share of volume a protocol keeps as revenue.
Window / stratumThe period is cut into equal strata; from each, one window of 40 consecutive blocks is downloaded in full.
95% intervalThe range holding the middle 95% of results when the sample is re-drawn from itself 2,000 times.
PooledA precision-weighted average of independent samples.
Out-of-sampleChecked on data that did not exist when the rule was written.
